How this is calculated

This site shows the most common eventual causes of death for a group of people who share your country, age, and sex. It is a population statistic, not a prediction about you as an individual.

What the number means

Pick a country, an age, and a sex, and the site answers one question: among people like that who are alive today, what share will eventually die of each cause? So a figure of "about 1 in 6" for cancer means that of a large group of similar people, roughly one in six are projected to die of cancer at some point, not that it will happen this year or to you.

Rank order is more reliable than the exact figures. The percentages are projections with real uncertainty, so treat them as approximate.

Where the data comes from

Two World Health Organization sources, covering 2000 to 2021:

  • Life tables from the WHO Global Health Observatory, which describe how likely people are to survive from one age to the next.
  • Cause-of-death data from the WHO Mortality Database, national records of what people actually died of, coded by cause. We reassign ill-defined codes onto real causes the way WHO does, so the ranking is not distorted.

How the projection works

We look at how death rates and the mix of causes have changed over the last two decades in each country, and carry those trends forward. Then, for a person alive at your age today, we follow a group like you through the rest of their lives on those projected rates, adding up the chance of eventually dying of each cause. Because everyone dies of something, the shares add up to 100 percent.

This is a genuine look forward, not a snapshot of who dies at your age now. A young adult is far more likely to die of an injury this year than of heart disease, but over a whole lifetime the long-term diseases dominate, and that is what the projection reflects.

Limitations

  • Seven countries only, chosen because their death records are detailed and near-complete.
  • Ages are grouped in five-year bands, and everyone over 85 is treated as one band.
  • The figures are model projections, most uncertain far into the future and for rare causes.
  • Causes are shown in groups; tap a group to see the specific causes within it.
  • Country-level figures hide large differences within a country.
